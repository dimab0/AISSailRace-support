Getting the fleet on screen

No boats showing

Shore AIS only hears boats within range of a receiver on land, usually 20 to 40 nautical miles off the coast, and it is sparser than a receiver on your own boat. Class B transponders, which most small boats carry, report every 30 seconds while moving. Offshore, use the boat network below. To see every screen with nobody around, turn on Setup → Demo race.

Connecting to the boat

Join the boat's Wi-Fi, turn on Setup → Boat network and pick a preset:

  • B&G, Simrad, Lowrance (GoFree): TCP 10110 at the plotter's address (often the Wi-Fi gateway, for example 192.168.76.1 on a Zeus 3S hotspot).
  • Yacht Devices gateways: TCP or UDP 1456.
  • Vesper, Digital Yacht and em-trak AIS with Wi-Fi: TCP 39150, or as the manual says.
  • Any gateway broadcasting NMEA 0183 over UDP: choose UDP and its port.

The app reads !AIVDM targets, your position (RMC, GGA, GLL, VTG) and the true wind (MWD, or MWV true with a heading).

How crossings are predicted

Both boats are projected forward on their present speed and course over ground, and the app finds the moment they are closest. Meetings within 600 m in the next 20 minutes are listed. Inside 150 m it adds the racing-rules call for the simple cases: opposite tacks, the port-tack boat keeps clear (rule 10); same tack, the windward boat keeps clear (rule 11). A tack, a shift or a late AIS report changes the picture, so treat it as a heads-up, not a ruling.

How tacks and gains are worked out

Each boat's course over ground is compared with the true wind: wind over the starboard side is starboard tack, and a change has to show in two reports at least 25 seconds apart. The wind is, in order: your instruments, a wind you set, the wind the fleet's tacking angles show, then the forecast. With a mark set, gains are measured toward it; without one, toward the wind upwind and away from it downwind, over the last 15 minutes. Leverage is the change in that measure if the wind turned 10° either way.
